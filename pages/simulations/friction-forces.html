The Physics

Static friction adjusts itself to prevent sliding, up to a maximum of μsN; once sliding starts, kinetic friction takes over with the roughly constant magnitude μkN (usually smaller — which is why things lurch when they break free).

On an incline, an object begins to slip when tan θ = μs, giving a simple way to measure the friction coefficient with nothing but a ramp.

Things to Try

  • Increase the applied force gradually and find the moment static friction gives way.
  • Tilt the incline until the block slips and compute μs from the angle.
