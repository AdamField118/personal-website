The Physics

Light passing near a black hole no longer travels in straight lines: it follows null geodesics of the Schwarzschild metric. Rays that pass close to the photon sphere at r = 3GM/c2 can be bent by large angles or even orbit the black hole before escaping, which is what produces the bright photon ring and the characteristic double image of the accretion disk.

The critical impact parameter is b = 3√3 GM/c2: rays aimed inside it are captured, rays outside it are deflected. This is the same physics (in the strong-field limit) as the weak gravitational lensing used to map dark matter.

Things to Try

  • Watch how the far side of the accretion disk appears bent above and below the shadow — that light is passing over and under the black hole.
  • Look for the thin photon ring just outside the shadow edge.
