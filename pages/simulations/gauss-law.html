The Physics

Gauss’ law states that the electric flux through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε0: ∮ E · dA = Qenc/ε0. Charges outside the surface contribute nothing — their field lines enter and leave in equal measure.

With enough symmetry (spheres, cylinders, planes) this single statement lets you find the field without integrating over the charge distribution — one of the most powerful shortcuts in electrostatics.

Things to Try

  • Move a charge in and out of the Gaussian surface and watch the flux jump.
  • Note the flux doesn’t change as you move a charge around inside the surface.
